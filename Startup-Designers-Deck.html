Case Study · Salesforce Field Service

Leading the design for an AI-agent based workforce planning system

How I helped Salesforce Field Service Planners see capacity gaps before they became emergencies.

Product Designer · Salesforce · 2025
Who is the user

What's a Planner?

Meet Sam, the Planner.

Salesforce Field Service powers enterprise companies including telecom crews and HVAC fleets with tools for their end-to-end operations.

Within large organizations, Operations Planners are responsible for making sure there are enough people to do the work in 1–3 months.

From feature to suite

A feature that evolved into a Suite

  • I owned the design for Capacity Limits at Salesforce Field Service.
  • The feature was showcased across the company and prioritized to evolve into a more holistic Planning Suite.

The Capacity Limits dashboard was Sam's first real signal. It proved the concept before we built the system.

Discovery & synthesis

My approach to understanding the Planner

I gathered existing research data

First I gathered existing research within the UX research organization as well as research done by an external third party and fed over 20 documents into a NotebookLM.

I also ran an affinity-mapping workshop

I gathered domain experts and users to understand how planners in specific industries were experiencing pains with planning.

Synthesis with Claude

I extracted user stories as well as a flowchart using Claude, and the Figma MCP within Claude.

To put the problem in clear terms:
0%
30% of total work hours are spent handling unplanned emergencies, disrupting planned work and reducing capacity.
0%
25% of companies still use spreadsheets for job scheduling, leading to error rates and information silos.

After synthesizing the data, I found the layered themes from each pointing to a design approach.

What Planners face
  • Power outages during storms — Mutual aid agreements between utilities share resources but spike demand.
  • Humanitarian aid during crises — Companies respond to worldwide SOS crises, such as climate emergencies.
  • Annual, advertised events — Marketing campaigns and holiday shopping cause service demand spikes.
How Sam is expected to fix it
  • Autonomous negotiation — Enable AI agents to interact as planners who negotiate between organizations.
  • Upskill management — Identify skill gaps and training needs; provide pathways for upskilling.
  • Allocation based on urgency — Measure and prioritize according to urgency of work and service needs.
Solution

An AI agent-based approach to capacity gap detection and resolution

I concluded that Sam should be able to land, understand her situation, and take action without friction. I designed an AI Gap Resolution Agent that can be entrusted to handle gap resolutions.
